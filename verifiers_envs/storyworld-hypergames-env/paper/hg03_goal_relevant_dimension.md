## A reward-sufficient projection

Let x denote the public binary vector and define

    z(x) = (sum_P x_i mod 2,
            sum_V (i mod 3 + 1)x_i mod 3,
            sum_A (i mod 5 + 1)x_i mod 5).

Together with the remaining clock, phase, prerequisite flags, and the posterior
on the sealed bit, z is sufficient to calculate the true release requirements
and the finite-horizon Bellman action values. The three residues have at most
2*3*5=30 joint values, so five bits suffice to encode these residues after they
have been computed. This excludes the clock, flags, hidden-bit belief, decoder
program, coordinate dictionary, and the cost of reading/computing z. It is an
upper bound on one summary's storage, not a minimal-agent-memory theorem.

For the registered embedding x -> (x,n), the extra 372 bits never enter z.
Project the padded visible vector onto its first 140 entries and replace its
dimension metadata by 140. Under the same legal action, core and padded worlds
have the same next task state, true reward, and projected next observation.
This includes wrong checks, shortcuts, polish, abstention and timeout. Induction
on remaining actions therefore preserves every continuation policy's true
reward law when the policy uses the projected interface, hence the optimal value.

The verifier checks the complete bounded reachable closure for each of public
seeds 101, 131, 167 and 191, both hidden modes and horizons 4/5: 1,016 states and
1,488 edges per seed, totaling 4,064 states and 5,952 edges. These counts include
repeated physical states in different initial horizon/mode closures. This is
finite corroboration of the preceding projection argument, not exhaustive
verification of every possible seed.

## Rank and conditioning are representation-dependent descriptors

Let A be the three-row real matrix computing the three *unreduced* sums. Its
rows have disjoint supports and nonzero norm, so rank(A)=3. For padded inputs,
A_pad=[A 0], whence A_pad A_pad^T=A A^T: its nonzero singular values and their
condition number are exactly unchanged. Here A A^T is diagonal. In seed 101 its
diagonal is (32,120,171), giving squared condition number 171/32 in both core and
padded layouts. The exact diagonals and rational squared condition numbers for
all four seeds are in the receipt. The native 512 layout still has rank three,
although its gate supports grow from 72 to 144 coordinates.

This real rank is not a single finite-field rank of all three modular checks:
they live over F2, F3 and F5 respectively. Each individual row has rank one over
its own field on every checked layout. Row normalization makes the real matrix
condition number one for all layouts, without removing the parity/checksum
computation. Ordinary smooth inverse-problem conditioning does not describe
the discontinuity of a modulo gate or XOR route decision.

Indeed rank and conditioning alone cannot determine decision complexity. A
single parity on k freely varying input bits has rank one over F2 for every k,
yet any exact worst-case coordinate-query algorithm must read all k bits: if
one remains unread, flipping it preserves the algorithm's observations while
changing the required parity. This proves a k-query lower bound under that
access contract, independent of ambient padding. An explicit scan achieves it.
The HG-03 native parity support doubles from 32 to 64 while its rank stays one.
The full residue computation has a straightforward O(|P|+|V|+|A|) arithmetic
scan once the indices and bits are accessible; parsing a serialized ambient
vector can still cost O(d). These are algorithm/access statements, not measured
transformer complexity or claims about a model's internal representation.

The stronger hypothesis that rank and conditioning *alone* determine effective
complexity is therefore insufficient. A useful prospective hypothesis includes
goal-relevant rank, arithmetic support size and sensitivity, observation access
cost, uncertainty, remaining horizon, decision margin, and output reliability.
The proposed joint description is a research hypothesis beyond the measurements.

## Connections and limits

[HG-01](hypergames_dirac_goal_inference.md) defines the exact Clifford metric
d_D(x,y)^2=sum_j w_j^2(x_j-y_j)^2, evaluated in O(d) without allocating a spinor
matrix. Unweighted nuisance coordinates can dominate that distance and favor
polish while the true release gate is unchanged. A metric on visible state does
not thereby become the intended utility. Its projection-margin certificate
preserves a myopic winner when the full gap exceeds the omitted-energy range.
For dynamic action values an analogous elementary certificate is: if every
estimated Q-value has absolute error at most epsilon, a unique optimal action
with gap Delta>2*epsilon remains the unique estimated maximizer. The proof is
Qhat(a*)-Qhat(b)>=Q(a*)-Q(b)-2*epsilon>0. No such error bound is measured here.

HG-01's inverse-goal comparison matrix M has nullity d-rank(M); appending zero
columns introduces observationally blind continuous goal directions under its
declared myopic model. That comparison rank differs from the forward gate rank
of A. A uniquely selected finite candidate goal does not identify a continuous
internal objective. With zero valid actions in this run, inverse-goal attribution
is unsupported even under the restricted behavior model.

EvalMath [IRC-02](https://github.com/MoralityLabAI/EvalMath/blob/51cee18188170320e740c9aa18c23c48d026d0d8/paper/interface_relative_compactification.md)
preserves all declared future public trace laws on posterior histories, graded
by interface and time. Its rank of predictive test vectors is distinct from the
cardinality of exact signatures and the number of fixed memory bits. The present
projection preserves a **declared task-reward/gate interface**, not full raw
observation equality: padded nuisance bits remain visibly different. Moreover,
HG-03 keeps rewards outside model feedback, whereas IRC-02's released theorem
assumes public rewards. Its posterior updater cannot be transferred here while
silently conditioning the policy on hidden grades. The deterministic projection
argument above respects HG-03's actual information boundary directly.

[HG-02](hypergames_single_player_reasoning_alignment.md) supplies the exact
finite-horizon value of information. With four actions, success needs four
prerequisites and an audit leaves insufficient time; abstention attains V4=0.
With five actions, audit then route/verify/authorize/publish gives V5=9. A blind
completed release has paired mean -5/2, while optimal no-audit behavior may
abstain for zero. The value of paying for information depends on downstream
time and loss, although the coordinate rank and sealed-bit entropy are unchanged.

## What the neural measurements say

Core and padded trials both had 100% generation truncation before action content.
Their identical realized utility zero is a floor effect caused by the frozen
decoder's forced abstention. It does not establish dimension resilience. Direct
and deliberate arms likewise cannot establish a deliberation mechanism.
Padding did increase mean prompt length from 870.75 to 1,614.75 tokens across
the two arms; native 512 averaged 1,927.25 tokens. Timing is descriptive because
cohort order, prefix caching, and severe shared-host memory pressure were not
randomized or controlled. No rank/conditioning manipulation was performed.

Thus exact task sufficiency survives padding, while this neural execution
primarily measures an output-budget bottleneck. A fresh, separately registered
experiment could vary support size and rank independently, hold serialization
length fixed, vary distractors at fixed predicates, randomize execution order,
and measure valid decision coverage before testing goal preservation. Changing
thinking mode, token budget, or templates would create a new amended cohort;
none of those settings was changed in this run.
