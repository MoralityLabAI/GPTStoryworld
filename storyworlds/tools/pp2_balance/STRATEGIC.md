# PP2-BAL-02: Nash-certified strategic reranking

Source: Cogentic, [An Exact Polynomial-Time Algorithm for 1/5-Approximate Nash Equilibria in Bimatrix Games](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/bimatrix_nash_equilibrium), pinned November 25, 2026. The source README claims a deterministic polynomial-time rational 1/5 approximation. Full paper PDF could not be retrieved as text in this session; the full theorem/Lean proof was not verified. The reported full algorithm has prohibitive theoretical constants. This module implements exact rational regret checks and the elementary kite correction, plus a bounded practical search inspired by multiscale/security anchoring. It is not a port of the complete MaximinKiteNE algorithm, and it may return unresolved.

## Native game reduction
An author-supplied sidecar assigns every decision encounter to exactly one of two declared actors, lists named pure policies with a fixed priority permutation of every authored option in every owned encounter, and supplies normalized rational terminal utility pairs in [0,1]. Select the highest-ranked visible option. A policy is drawn once at the root; it may react to the encounter ID and legal action mask only. Native StoryworldEngine selects reactions and applies effects, preserving signed p/p2 values, full keyrings, clipping, visibility, routing and ties. No automatic assignment of hidden-state utilities; no knowledge of hidden coordinates beyond the reader's visible option mask. Pairwise enumeration yields a restricted rational bimatrix game and exact terminal IDs. Repeated encounters use the same priority order. Missing utility, dead end, horizon, incomplete policy or budget overflow fails closed. Third parties are part of the deterministic environment. Full extensive-form/hypergame Nash claims require expanded information-set policies, consistent information sets, explicit chance and full deviation coverage.

For matrices R,C and independent mixed root policies x,y, certify
rA=max_i (Ry)_i-x^T R y and rB=max_j (x^T C)_j-x^T C y.
The verifier independently reconstructs rational simplex mass, all pure best responses, payoffs and regrets using BigInt fractions. Both must be <=1/5. A certificate is conditional on the declared policy roster and terminal utilities, and exact rational only conditional on the reader's floating-point transitions. No claim of global equilibrium for the narrative. Uniform *root-policy* regret is distinct from PP2-BAL-01's uniform *legal-option* ending law.

## Bounded solver
Uniform and pure profiles, exact two-support indifference intersections, and optional KITE-style projection and multiscale updates. For rA>s=1/5 and 4rA+rB<=1, mix x toward its pure best reply by alpha=1-s/rA; row regret becomes s and column regret is at most (1-alpha)rB+alpha<=s. Symmetric case for column. Every proposed correction is independently checked. Security anchors maximize minimum payoff over the finite visited strategy pool and are lower bounds, not exact LP maximin values. Three rates 1/2, 1/128, 1/8192 and weight 1/4 are practical choices, without a termination theorem. Every accepted candidate has an explicit certificate. Caps count unique strategy evaluations; support-only and KITE are matched by cap, not actual calls or elapsed time.

## Authoring
rerank(Engine, balanceAPI, world, approvedSites, target, strategicManifest, config) runs the existing author search, extracts games for the exact same candidate portfolio, and returns author_best and strategic_best separately. Rerank requires a certified profile and orders eligible candidates by original author loss, strategic ending TV, regret, edit count and deterministic key. If none qualify, strategic_best=null. The author-best may remain preferable; the tool does not write worlds or change scripts. A selected equilibrium's ending distribution is computed exactly from independent root mixtures. Multiple equilibria may produce very different distributions: this selected profile is a witness, not a robustness envelope. Future work: equilibrium selection bounds, best responses outside the roster, explicit information histories, chance, author-approved real utility sidecars, held-out structural worlds and DataGoblin receipts.
